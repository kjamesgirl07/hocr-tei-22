About this edition

This is version 2.0 of a TEI P5 encoding of coxswain Pete Cipollone's in-boat recording from the US Rowing men's Championship Eight at the Head of the Charles Regatta on 19 October 1997. The crew started first, finished first in 13:58.9, and set a course record that lasted until 2017. row2k.com published the audio and transcript in 2010, with the audio digitized by Cipollone and Oli Rosenbladt.

Version 1.0, the original student encoding, is kept unchanged in original/. Both versions validate against tei_all. The revision is about meaning, accuracy and reuse, not schema errors.

Recommendations

Items marked done are carried out in this version. Items marked next are proposed for future work.

1

Correct the factual notes done

The bow pair note placed the pair "in the very back of the boat". The bow pair are seats 1 and 2, at the front of the boat. The closing note called the regatta a "32 year old race" and gave no time. The 1997 race was the 32nd regatta, because 1996 was cancelled by a storm, and the official time was 13:58.9, a course record. The note "36 is their pace" now explains stroke rate in strokes per minute. Every correction is tagged <note type="correction"> and cites its source.

2

Give the header real responsibility and source metadata done

<author>NA</author> and the listing of Cipollone and Rosenbladt as editors are replaced with <respStmt> entries for speaker, digitizers, publisher and encoder. A full <bibl> records the row2k feature with its URL and date, and <recordingStmt> describes the audio. The header also adds a rights statement, <editionStmt> and <revisionDesc>.

3

Encode speech as speech done

The text is a recording, not prose, so <p> plus italic "stage directions" becomes the TEI spoken-text module. Each call is a <u who="#cipollone">. Voice changes become <shift feature="voice" new="whisper"/>, and crowd noise and bridge echoes become <incident>. The starting official's words are no longer an anonymous italic paragraph. They are attributed with who="#official".

<!-- 1.0 -->
<p><hi rend="italic">(whispers)</hi></p>
<p>Pushing, you're right on 36.</p>

<!-- 2.0 -->
<u who="#cipollone" ana="#ct-rate"><shift feature="voice" new="whisper"/>
  Pushing, you're right on <measure type="strokeRate" unit="spm" quantity="36">36</measure>.</u>
4

Identify the people and places done

row2k gives the crew bow to stern. <particDesc> now lists all nine people with seats and colleges, and each name in the transcript points to that list, nicknames included ("Philthy" is Phil Henry and "Giboon" is Jonathan Gibbons, both marked as editorial inferences). <settingDesc> lists the course bridges in order, which links the calls to the river. See Crew & course.

5

Use @ana and notes for what they are for done

In 1.0, @ana held free-text comments such as ana="what does five mean?". TEI defines @ana as pointers to interpretations. Those comments are now typed notes (editorial, query, correction, context), and @ana points to a seven-category <taxonomy> of call types. That makes the transcript filterable and countable.

6

Move styling into the header done

Values like style="color #FFEF12" and style="font-size 24pt" are not valid CSS, because they lack the colon. They are replaced with <rendition> definitions in <tagsDecl>, referenced with rendition="#shout" and rendition="#context".

7

Build a glossary instead of scattered footnotes done

Twenty-five rowing terms are defined once in <back>, and each use points to them with <term ref="#gl-…">. On the site they appear as hover and keyboard-focus tooltips. The 1.0 note on "Fi' chaa", where the note text ran into the transcript line, is fixed.

8

Stop hotlinking images done

One <graphic> pointed at a Britannica web page rather than an image file. The others hotlinked copyrighted photos from third-party sites. They are replaced with original SVG diagrams of the boat and course. Any photos added later should be openly licensed and carry a <figDesc> for screen readers.

9

State a consistent profanity policy done

1.0 censored some words but not others. row2k left the profanity in deliberately. The policy is now declared in <editorialDecl>, every expletive is marked <seg type="expletive">, and readers can mask them with a toggle, which suits classroom use.

10

Turn the encoding into data done

Stroke rates, elapsed times and distances are encoded with <measure>. scripts/build.py builds every page of this site from the TEI, including the analysis charts, so the site cannot drift from the XML.

11

Synchronize with the audio next

Ask row2k for permission, then add a <timeline> with @start and @end on each <u> so the edition can highlight the line being spoken. A first anchor is already known: the "here comes a headwind" call is at 15:53 of the full recording. row2k has had an unauthorized copy of the audio removed from YouTube, so link to the audio rather than rehosting it.

12

Encode the full 27 minutes next

This excerpt stops at 12:40. The full row2k transcript includes the row to the start, the officials' clock confusion and the finish, where the cox calls 14:03 against the official 13:58.9. That gap between the two times deserves an editorial note.

13

Add a comparison text next

row2k also published Seth Bauer's recording from the same day, coxing the ten-year reunion of the 1987 world champion eight. Encoding it with the same taxonomy would allow a real comparison of two elite coxswains.

14

Resolve the open queries next

Several readings remain uncertain: "A** to ankles", "speed five", which turn "the big dog" refers to, and the two nicknames. A short email or oral-history interview with Cipollone or a crew member could settle them. Record the answers with @resp and @cert.

15

Code every call, and check agreement next

Pure rhythm calls ("Push, send") are still uncoded. Coding all of them, with a second coder working independently, would make the call-type counts publishable.

16

Automate quality checks next

Add a Schematron rule that every @ref and @who resolves to an @xml:id, and a GitHub Actions workflow that validates the TEI and rebuilds the site on every push.

17

Map the course next

Replace the schematic with a real map, such as GeoJSON with Leaflet. Give each bridge <geo> coordinates and place markers where each call was made.
