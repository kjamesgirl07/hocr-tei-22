      <div type="glossary" xml:id="glossary">
        <head>Glossary of rowing terms and calls</head>
        <list type="gloss">
          <label xml:id="gl-send">send</label><item>Paired with "push" or "legs" to set the rhythm: push hard with the legs, then send the boat forward so it runs freely between strokes.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-chaa">chaa</label><item>A sharp sound the coxswain makes on the drive to mark the rhythm and add energy. Coxswains use many calls, phrases and sounds, depending on the moment in the race and what the rowers need.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-shift">shift</label><item>A change of stroke rate. Here the crew raises the rate after the start sprint or settles it to a lower rate. "In two we shift" warns the crew that the change comes in two strokes.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-rate">stroke rate (cadence)</label><item>Strokes per minute (spm). Calls such as 36, 39 and "35 and a half" report the crew's rate.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-easy-all">easy all</label><item>A call to ease off or stop the current effort.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-paddle">paddle</label><item>Rowing lightly, with little pressure.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-weigh-enough">weigh enough</label><item>A traditional call to stop rowing. The rowers rest their oars on the water.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-half-power">half power</label><item>Rowing at about half pressure, often used to settle the boat.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-build">build</label><item>Raising the pressure over a set number of strokes ("three to build").</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-stall">stall</label><item>When a crew loses speed or momentum.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-walk">walk / walking away</label><item>Gaining on another crew, or extending a lead, seat by seat.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-drive">drive</label><item>The working phase of the stroke, from the catch to the finish, when the legs, back and arms move the boat.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-catch">catch</label><item>The moment the blade enters the water. "Catches in" asks for quick, together blade entry.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-finish">finish</label><item>The end of the drive, when the blade comes out of the water.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-spacing">spacing</label><item>The run of the boat between strokes, and the ratio of drive to recovery. "Back out to even spacing" asks the crew to restore its usual rhythm after a push.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-flat-water">flat water</label><item>Calm water with no chop, good for long, powerful strokes.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-headwind">headwind</label><item>Wind blowing against the boat's direction of travel. The standard technical response is to sit up and drive.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-sit">sit (do not sit)</label><item>Easing off while appearing to row. "Do not sit" demands full effort on every stroke.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-commit">commit / commitment</label><item>A planned, sustained push over a set distance or time, here one minute through a bridge.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-seat-race">seat race</label><item>A selection test in which two rowers swap boats so coaches can compare them. Calling the race Callahan's "seat race" tells him his seat is on the line.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-rudder">on the rudder</label><item>The coxswain is steering, which adds drag. The crew should stay steady through it.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-port">port</label><item>The rowers whose oars are on the boat's left side, as seen by the coxswain facing forward. A turn "to port" bends left.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-starboard">starboard</label><item>The rowers whose oars are on the boat's right side, as seen by the coxswain.</item>
          <label xml:id="gl-bow-pair">bow pair</label><item>Seats 1 and 2, the two rowers nearest the bow, which is the front of the boat. Here they are Callahan and Holbrook.<note type="correction">Version 1.0 said the bow pair sit "in the very back of the boat". The bow is the front of the boat, but it is behind the rowers, who face the stern.</note></item>
          <label xml:id="gl-rigger">rigger</label><item>The metal or carbon frame that holds the oarlock away from the hull.</item>
        </list>
